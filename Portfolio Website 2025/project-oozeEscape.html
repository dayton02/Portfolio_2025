Ooze Escape

Custom Engine C++

The Challenge

Ooze Escape lives or dies on a single mechanic feeling right: Gloo, a soft-body slime, deforms and squeezes through the environment using its mass and shape. Early in production, the soft-body was the biggest technical risk on the project. The slime jittered, vibrated, and flew apart under force, and that instability leaked into other systems, breaking collisions and the gameplay that depended on it.

What I Built

As the physics and backend programmer, I owned the soft-body simulation inside our team's custom C++ engine, alongside collision detection and the JSON-based save/load systems that persisted level state.

How I Solved It

Rather than invent a soft-body model from scratch, I studied how Unity handles the same problem and rebuilt its approach in our engine. I modeled the slime as a set of point masses held together by spring constraints, mirroring Unity's SpringJoint2D: each point is pulled toward its neighbors so the body keeps its slime shape while still deforming naturally under force and collision. Tuning the spring stiffness and damping turned an unstable, exploding blob into the controllable, springy character the entire game is built around.
